01 / 13 Introduction

Scan waste.

WasteWise is a Final Year Project on automated waste understanding: a two-stage deep-learning pipeline (YOLO26m finds every object, a ConvNeXt classifier verifies the material), backed by a classical machine-learning branch for explainable evidence. Every number on this site survives a leakage-audited test set.

The problem, the goal, the system

Manual waste sorting is slow and error-prone, and recycling streams are easily contaminated. WasteWise localizes each waste object in a photo, verifies its material, and routes it to the correct bin, with an honest, audited account of what works and what does not.

Objective 1 Detect & classify waste in real photos

Localize every object with YOLO26m, then verify the material of each crop across 7 classes with a dedicated classifier.

Objective 2 Evaluate honestly

Audit the evaluation sets with perceptual hashing, quarantine leaked images, and report only clean-test numbers.

Objective 3 Explainable evidence

Keep a classical ML branch on 637 handcrafted features beside the deep pipeline, so every decision has an interpretable baseline.

02 / 13 Dataset Overview

Six public sources, merged into one detection dataset.

No single public dataset covers waste detection well: TACO has real litter photos but sparse labels, TrashNet has clean studio crops but no boxes, WaDaBa focuses on plastic subtypes, and GINI frames garbage-vs-clean scenes. The project merges them, plus Roboflow garbage-classification sources and the PlastOPol field dataset, into one YOLO-format dataset with 7 material classes. After tuning, the working dataset holds 31,379 images and 67,385 boxes (24,668 / 3,298 / 3,413 train/valid/test).

i

Six sources, two acquisition regimes. Of the six merged sources, TrashNet and WaDaBa contribute studio-style imagery; TACO, GINI, the Roboflow projects and PlastOPol contribute real-world scenes. (The classifier’s crop benchmarks later add Kaggle Garbage Classification as their studio bucket.) Every headline in this deck is reported against that studio/field divide, because pooled averages hide it.

03 / 13 Image Preprocessing

From six raw sources to one clean, balanced dataset.

Merging is not enough. The raw union is imbalanced, contains invalid and tiny boxes, and duplicates the same photos across sources. The preprocessing pass removes invalid label rows and exact duplicates, drops degenerate boxes, re-splits train/valid/test by dominant class, and caps per-class counts so no material dominates. At inference time the same discipline applies: each detected box is cropped with 10 px padding, boxes under 24×24 px are rejected, and each crop is summarized into 637 handcrafted features (8 spatial + 9 FFT + 44 color + 576 HOG) for the ML branch.

04 / 13 Exploratory Data Analysis

What the data actually looks like, including its bugs.

EDA on the merged dataset answered three questions: is the class balance usable, how small are the objects, and, most importantly, can the test set be trusted? The third question found a real evaluation bug: perceptual hashing showed 2,406 test images (43%) of an earlier classifier evaluation set also appeared in training, inflating its accuracy from an honest 91.77% to 94.30%. All headline numbers on this site are measured after that leakage was quarantined.

!

The number that almost shipped. 94.30% looked like a finished result. It wasn’t: 43% of that test set was quietly sitting in training too, recompressed or resized but still the same photo. An honest number that survives an audit (91.77%) beats a flattering one that doesn’t, so every metric on this site is measured after the leaked images were found and removed.

05 / 13 Model Pipeline

Localize first, classify second.

Stage 1 finds objects, which is what YOLO is good at. Stage 2 verifies the material of each crop, which is what a dedicated classifier is good at, and filters false alarms with a Background veto class.

S1
Upload image

A JPG, PNG, or WEBP photo of waste. Saved scans stay in browser history for review and hard-case collection.

S2
Stage 1: YOLO26m localization

YOLO26m proposes a box around every candidate object. 100-epoch retrain, promoted from YOLO26s for higher field recall and precision: 74.9% mAP50, 83.4% precision on clean validation.

S3
Crop extraction & size filter

Each kept box is cropped with 10 px padding; boxes smaller than 24×24 px are rejected before classification.

S4
Stage 2: crop verification

A ConvNeXt-Tiny + 637-handcrafted-feature classifier re-checks every crop across 7 classes, including a Background veto. Fine-tuned on the detector's own crops so training matches production.

S5
Score fusion, context prior & bin routing

Per-box score fuses detector and classifier confidences, a scene-context prior adjusts the class scores, and the dominant material is routed to its bin by a direct material→bin lookup.

i

The Background veto is the load-bearing wall. Section 6’s detector confusion matrix shows the detector’s errors are misses and false alarms, not material mix-ups — a second stage that re-judges every crop, plus a Background class that can veto, is aimed at exactly that error profile.

06 / 13 Model Training

Training curves and confusion matrices.

The deployed detector is a 100-epoch YOLO26m retrain, promoted from YOLO26s after it won on field recall. The deployed classifier is ConvNeXt-Tiny + 637 features, fine-tuned on the detector's own crops so training matches production. The comparison models below (EfficientNetB0, ResNet50, MobileNetV2, tiny CNN) were trained on the same balanced crop benchmark to justify the final choice. ResNet50 and MobileNetV2 were retrained for 15 epochs so their curves show where each model stops learning and starts memorizing; the earlier 2-epoch runs were too short to read.

i

Read the curve, not just the final number. ResNet50’s validation loss bottoms out at epoch 3 of 15; everything after that is memorization wearing the costume of more training. MobileNetV2 buys three extra epochs before the same thing happens, at a tenth of the parameters, which is why parameter count alone never settles a model choice here.

08 / 13 Experimental Results

What the experiments actually changed.

The headline experiments: fine-tuning the classifier on the detector's own crops lifted production-distribution accuracy from 76.9% to 88.9% without losing clean-crop accuracy (92.9% → 92.93%, unchanged within noise). Promoting YOLO26s to a 100-epoch YOLO26m retrain lifted field recall from 70.9% to 78.1% and field small-object recall from 58.6% to 68.2% at the same threshold. Hard-negative mining on new field data (PlastOPol) lifted held-out small-object recall from 38.6% to 54.7%. New data, not architecture, was the lever.

✓

Every lift in this section came from data, not architecture: crop fine-tuning +12.0 points, hard-negative mining +16.1 points, the YOLO26m promotion +7.2 points of field recall. That pattern — data is the lever — is the project’s main lesson, and the new training round in Section 10 confirms it from the architecture side.

09 / 13 Model Comparison

Classical vs deep, and deep vs deep.

Two comparisons justify the final design. Classical vs deep: the best tree ensemble on 637 handcrafted features reaches 73.8% on the balanced benchmark: genuinely useful, fully explainable, but ~20 points behind the deployed deep classifier. Deep vs deep: EfficientNetB0 beat ResNet50 by 2.1 points at a sixth of the weight size, and was later replaced by the ConvNeXt-Tiny + features model that survives the leakage audit at 92.93%.

!

The gotcha the classical branch exposed. Train the 637-feature model on studio photos and test it on real-world field photos, and accuracy falls off a cliff: from 79.8% in-domain down to 39.0%. Studio and field photos are the same materials lit and framed differently, and that gap is the whole reason field-domain data and matched operating points matter more than another point of benchmark accuracy.

Deep architecture shoot-out same data, same split

EfficientNetB0: 94.3% at 16.8 MB vs ResNet50's 92.2% at 95.1 MB.

MobileNetV2 91.4% (9.6 MB, 2.4M params) · ResNet50 92.2% (95.1 MB, 23.8M params) · EfficientNetB0 94.3% (16.8 MB, 4.2M params). Sizes are FP32 weights derived from the parameter count, so the three are compared on one basis. Compound scaling wins the accuracy-per-megabyte race. These figures predate the leakage audit; the audited successor is the deployed ConvNeXt at 92.93% on the clean test set.

Classical ML baseline explainable evidence

73.8%: ExtraTrees on 637 handcrafted features.

8 spatial + 9 FFT + 44 color + 576 HOG features per crop. Kept for comparison and explainability evidence beside the deep pipeline, and it is the branch that exposed the domain-shift problem (39.0% cross-domain).

10 / 13 New Training Round

The retrain round: seven detectors, six backbones.

Two fresh benchmark campaigns, run after the main pipeline shipped. First, a matched-budget detector sweep: seven architectures (YOLO26m, YOLOv8m, YOLO11m, RT-DETR-l, Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, FCOS) trained on the same merged dataset under the same 30-epoch budget. Second, a Stage-2 backbone rematch: six frozen backbones, each paired with the same 637 handcrafted features and the same MLP head, against the deployed ConvNeXt-Tiny classifier on the leakage-quarantined hard-case split. Every chart below renders live from the run artifacts in runs/dl/; the confusion matrices were recomputed from the stored test features and verified against each run’s reported accuracy.

i

The whole round in one sentence. RT-DETR-l tops the detector sweep (56.2% test mAP50, +8.0 points over YOLO26m) but costs 2× the training time; Swin-Tiny and ConvNeXtV2 edge the deployed classifier by +0.7 points at a 12–30% CPU-latency premium in matched-session measurements; MobileNetV3-L gives up 1.8 points but runs roughly 2.7× faster. Nothing was promoted: the deployed pair still holds the best accuracy-per-millisecond trade, and that is the honest reading of both boards.

Same merged dataset, same 30 epochs, wildly different outcomes.

🎤Speaker narrative

Two stories in one chart: who wins, and how far every bar falls from validation to test.

Point at the gap between each model’s pale bar and solid bar before naming any winner.

Say every architecture drops 15–24 points from validation to the unseen-session test set. That gap is a property of the dataset’s domain shift, not of any model, and no architecture in the sweep closes it. Within that ceiling, RT-DETR-l is the clear winner at 56.2% test mAP50, eight points over the deployed YOLO26m at the same budget.

If asked why YOLO26m is still the deployed detector: the sweep is a 30-epoch matched-budget comparison; the production model is a 100-epoch retrain tuned to a field-recall operating point, and RT-DETR-l costs 3.2 training hours against YOLO26m’s 1.6 for the same 30 epochs. RT-DETR is the strongest candidate for a future promotion, not evidence the current one was wrong.

Per-epoch validation mAP50 for the four runs with curve logs (torchvision runs log only final metrics). The draw-in animation replays the 30-epoch schedule.

!

The per-class floor is the real finding. On the merged dataset’s unseen-session test split, Glass AP collapses for every architecture (0.03–0.29) and Organic never passes 0.18. Seven architectures, one shared failure: that is a dataset property. More field-condition Glass and Organic examples — TACO- and PlastOPol-style captures — would move these numbers; another architecture will not.

🎤Speaker narrative

The deployed model survives its own rematch, and the two challengers that beat it pay for the privilege.

Point at the deployed row first, then the two rows above it.

Say Swin-Tiny and ConvNeXtV2 win by seven-tenths of a point, but both pay for it in CPU latency: measured against the deployed model in each candidate’s own benchmark session, Swin-Tiny costs +7 ms (+12%) and ConvNeXtV2 +15 ms (+30%) per crop, for a margin inside day-to-day noise. MobileNetV3-L is the interesting one: 92.1% at 18.5 ms, roughly 2.7× faster than production, which makes it the obvious pick if this ever moves on-device.

If asked about EfficientNetV2-S at 80.2%: it is uniformly weak across all seven classes on both validation and test, so it is not a bad split or a broken run. Its frozen features simply separate this domain poorly without fine-tuning; the benchmark freezes every backbone on purpose, and this is the failure mode that policy exposes.

🎤Speaker narrative

Six matrices, one shared shape: strong diagonals, and the same two leaks the deployed model has.

Point at the Plastic→Background cell, then the Paper↔Cardboard pair, on whichever model is selected.

Say the error structure barely changes across backbones. Plastic leaking into Background and Paper trading with Cardboard are the two residual confusions in every matrix, the same pair the deployed model shows in Section 6. Swapping the backbone changes how often those cells fire, not which cells fire, which is more evidence the remaining errors live in the data, not the architecture.

If asked how these matrices were produced when the runs only saved metrics: the benchmark caches every test crop’s features, so the saved MLP head was re-run over the cached features and the predictions cross-checked against each run’s logged accuracy before rendering.

11 / 13 Discussion

Measured limits, and what was done about them.

Domain shift Partly fixed, partly open

Fixed: the classifier was fine-tuned on the detector's own crops, lifting production-distribution accuracy from 76.9% to 88.9%. The detector was re-tuned for the field domain too: lowering the confidence threshold and hard-negative mining on new field data (PlastOPol) lifted held-out field small-object recall from 38.6% to 54.7%, at a small cost to studio mAP50 (72.1% → 70.9%). Open: on fully unseen capture sessions the detector still drops to 0.444 mAP50; the gap is narrowing with new data, not fully closed.

Tiny objects Resolution ruled out; new data confirmed as the lever

A 960px training run was evaluated: validation unchanged, unseen-batch recall up 3.9 points, not enough to chase. A later hard-negative mining fine-tune on new field data confirmed the real lever: small-object recall on that held-out domain rose 38.6% to 54.7% from new data alone, no resolution or architecture change. Small objects remain the main source of misses; training-data diversity is the binding constraint.

Material-first routing No disposability judgment

The app reports what an object is made of and routes that material to its bin. It does not judge whether the item should actually be thrown away; that waste-state decision was removed because there was no dataset to train it reliably.

Method note. Metrics on this site come from the leakage-audited evaluation sets. The audit scripts, quarantine manifest, and reports live in the project repository. The Stage-2 architecture shoot-out (EfficientNetB0, ResNet50, MobileNetV2) is measured on the balanced crop benchmark that predates the quarantine, so all three share one test set and stay comparable to each other, but not to the 92.93% clean-test headline.

12 / 13 Conclusion & Future Work

A working, honestly-measured waste scanner.

The project delivers a deployed two-stage pipeline (92.93% clean-test material accuracy, 74.9% detector mAP50) and, just as importantly, a worked example of finding and fixing one's own evaluation bug. The classical branch keeps every headline claim explainable; the audit trail keeps it honest.
